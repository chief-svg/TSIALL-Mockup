Rules of the road

  • Payoffs are the live balance, not the round number. Open the card app that day and pay what it shows; the figures here include estimated interest to the date.
  • No new charges on any card that carries a balance. None of them has a grace period, so every purchase accrues interest from the day it posts. Day-to-day spending comes out of checking with the debit card.
  • Paying a statement in full does not stop the interest while a remainder stays on the card; only a $0 balance does. Kill cards whole, highest rate first, and pay just the minimum on the ones still waiting.
  • Rent safety on Sep 27. Checking sits near $4,300 after the payments ($3,000 after the Sep 28 student loan + IRS) before Jessica’s Sep 30 check. If rent could draft before her pay posts, make only the three payoffs on the 27th ($20,330) and send the $2,200 to Biz Plat on the 30th once her check clears. Cost: about $6 of interest.
  • Expect a trailing-interest charge on each card the statement after its payoff, typically $30–$100. Pay it when it posts — the grace period only returns once a statement closes at $0; the Jan 27 line sweeps whatever remains.
  • Loans need a payoff quote. SoFi and Wells Fargo charge per-diem interest; get the quote a few days before Dec 27 and Jan 27 and pay that figure. The IRS balance updates daily in your online account; pay what it shows on Jan 27.
  • The app is the truth. The Payoff page recomputes the funds check from live balances on every open; this sheet is the plan as of today.
