Rules of the road

  • Payoffs are the live balance, not the round number. Open the card app that day and pay what it shows; the figures here include estimated interest to the date.
  • Two kinds of card. The Citi 8018 and 3208 are in grace (their statements show $0 interest): pay the statement balance in full by the due date and nothing on it ever pays interest. Amex Biz Plat, Biz Gold, Sapphire, and the personal Platinum from Sep 21, are carrying: interest accrues daily on the whole balance, so they die whole-card, highest rate first, with only the minimum on the ones still waiting.
  • Living goes on the 8018 and nowhere else. Its statement closes on the 24th and is due on the 20th; pay it in full from Jessica’s 15th check every month. Miss one payment in full and the whole statement is charged interest from the start of its cycle.
  • No new charges on any carrying card.
  • Sep 16–20 is tight. The $3,963.71 leaves about $370 in checking after SoFi. If Jessica’s check lands short or late, pay what you can by Friday Sep 18 and know the 8018 then loses its grace (about $10/day until it is paid). Rent on Oct 1 comes from Jessica’s Sep 30 check.

  • Expect a trailing-interest charge on each card the statement after its payoff, typically $30–$100. Pay it when it posts — the grace period only returns once a statement closes at $0; the Jan 27 line sweeps whatever remains.
  • Loans need a payoff quote. SoFi and Wells Fargo charge per-diem interest; get the quote a few days before Dec 27 and Jan 27 and pay that figure. The IRS balance updates daily in your online account; pay what it shows on Jan 27.
  • The app is the truth. The Payoff page recomputes the funds check from live balances on every open; this sheet is the plan as of today.
